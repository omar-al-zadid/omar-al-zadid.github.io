Teaching

Helping students understand, recognize, and apply.

My teaching philosophy centers on three outcomes: clear and durable understanding, recognition of relevance, and application through reasoning. I pursue these outcomes through structured teaching, repeated engagement, realistic applications, and gradually increasing student independence.

Teaching Philosophy

Clear & Durable Understanding

I structure course material around clear learning goals, break complex ideas into manageable steps, and reinforce important concepts through repeated engagement, checks for understanding, and connections across topics.

Recognition of Relevance

I use cases, realistic business situations, and applied activities to help students recognize familiar concepts when they appear in unfamiliar settings rather than simply recalling textbook definitions.

Application Through Reasoning

I want students to become independent problem solvers. They practice selecting appropriate methods, explaining their reasoning, considering alternatives, and applying what they know beyond familiar problem formats.

How I Put It Into Practice

Structure & Reinforcement

I organize lectures from foundational ideas toward more complex applications, regularly check understanding, and revisit difficult concepts when necessary. I also summarize key ideas at the end of class and reconnect them with later material so students encounter important concepts more than once.

Learning Through Context

I use cases and applied exercises to help students recognize concepts in different settings. Examples include short Tableau exercises, classifying quality-cost scenarios, and activities that allow students to discover statistical patterns before formal concepts are introduced.

Guidance Toward Independence

I initially model how to approach a problem, then gradually reduce guidance as students gain confidence. Students are encouraged to justify their methods, consider alternative approaches, and develop their own problems and solutions.

Examples From the Classroom
Discovering Sampling Distributions

In Business Statistics, student groups repeatedly draw small random samples and calculate sample means. Once the class can see the distribution emerging, I formally introduce sampling distributions and the Central Limit Theorem, then reconnect those ideas later to confidence intervals and hypothesis testing.

Recognizing Quality Costs

In Operations Management, students work with scenarios involving prevention, appraisal, internal failure, and external failure costs. Groups classify each situation and compare their reasoning, helping them recognize concepts beyond the examples in which they were originally introduced.

Creating Decision Problems

In Decision Analysis, students develop their own multi-stage decision problems—preferably from their own experiences—and solve them using decision trees. This requires them to decide how the method applies rather than simply reproducing a familiar exercise.

Teaching Innovation & Scholarship
Teaching Research in Progress

Practicing Contract Negotiations with AI

with Charles L. Munson and Jonathan Jackson

This project examines how AI can support practice-based learning of complex negotiation skills. Students negotiate purchasing contracts with AI tools acting as sellers while we examine how effectively they incorporate important contractual elements into their decisions.

The exercise is being integrated into Operations Management instruction to explore how AI-supported practice can help students develop judgment, adaptability, and negotiation skills.

Continuous Improvement

Understanding Students

I use a pre-course survey to better understand students' backgrounds, preparation, and expectations before the course develops.

Reflective Teaching

I maintain a teaching journal to record what worked, what did not, and what I would change. This helps me treat teaching as an iterative process rather than a fixed routine.

Responding to Students

I use student questions, feedback, and classroom responses to adjust explanations, pacing, examples, and instructional approaches when needed.
